Picking your first weights
You've no idea what you can lift yet, and that's fine — week 1 is data collection, not training.

Set 1 of anything: pick a weight you're confident you could do about 15 reps with, and stop at the top of the range. Too light on purpose.
Set 2: if that felt easy, go up. Keep going up until the last two reps of a set are genuinely hard but your form doesn't break.
That's your number. Write it in the box. By session three you'll have a real starting point for every lift.

The target feel is 1–2 reps left in the tank at the end of each set. Not failure. Grinding to failure every set on four sessions a week while eating 600 calories under maintenance is how you end up injured and demoralised by October.
How the weight goes up
Double progression. Reps first, then weight. It's the simplest system that actually works and it removes all the guessing.
1

2
Next week: 10, 10, 9. Still 20 kg.
3
Week after: 10, 10, 10 — top of the range on every set. Now add the increment listed under that exercise, and start again at the bottom of the range.

Stuck at the same numbers for three sessions running? Don't force it. Check your sleep and your protein first — at a 600 calorie deficit, stalling usually means recovery, not effort.
Expect the weights to climb fast for six to eight weeks (that's your nervous system learning, not muscle), then slow right down. Slower progress while you're losing fat is normal and completely fine — holding your strength during a cut is a win in itself.
Deload · every 8th week
On weeks 8, 16 and 24, do 2 sets instead of 3 and drop every weight by about 40%. Keep the treadmill exactly as prescribed.

It will feel like cheating. It isn't — it's when the adaptations from the previous seven weeks actually land, and it's why people who deload are still training in year two. You're doing this on top of a nine-hour standing shift seven days a week; you need it more than most.

Rules that keep you training in six months
·
Weights before the run. Always. Lifting on legs you've just run 30 minutes on is how backs and knees go, and you'll lift less for no benefit.
·
Rest properly between sets. The rest times aren't padding — cutting a 2 minute rest to 45 seconds means you lift less weight, which means less muscle kept. Use the time to log your last set.
·
Form beats weight, every time. Nobody at the gym is impressed and nobody is watching. Film a set on your phone occasionally and compare it to the cue.
·
Machine taken? Every exercise has an alternative listed — tap the name. Never stand around waiting; it wrecks the session timing.
·
Seven-day work week. If you're genuinely wrecked, drop session D and keep A, B and C. Three sessions you turn up to beat four you resent.
·
Soreness 24–48h after is normal and fades within a fortnight. Sharp pain, joint pain, or pain during the movement is not — stop and get it looked at.
·
Shoes. You're about to run 120 times over five months at 89 kg. Get properly fitted running shoes before week 5, and replace them at around 700 km.

Skip test · do this before week 1
You have an MSc and a bootcamp behind you, so some of the foundations will be revision and some won't. Don't guess — check. Open a blank file and actually do these, no googling, no AI. Whatever you can't do in a couple of minutes is what your foundation weeks are for.
Py
Write a function with a default argument and **kwargs. Write a list comprehension with a condition in it. Write a class with __init__ and one method. Catch a specific exception, not a bare except. Create a virtualenv and freeze a requirements.txt.
SQL
Explain when a LEFT JOIN gives you rows an INNER JOIN doesn't, and why a WHERE on the right-hand table silently turns one into the other. Write a GROUP BY with a HAVING. Write ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY ...). Rewrite a subquery as a CTE.
pd
groupby().agg() with two different functions. merge two frames and explain the how argument. Fill missing values three different ways and say why you'd pick each. Reshape wide to long.
Stat
Say in one sentence what a p-value actually means. Explain when the median beats the mean. Explain overfitting to someone non-technical. Explain why a correlation of 0.9 might mean nothing.
Scoring: all four blocks clean → skip to week 8 and start AI literacy. One shaky block → do that block's weeks only. Two or more → do all of weeks 1–7 properly. There's no prize for skipping, and everything from stage 2 onwards silently assumes this.
Why AI engineering, and why this order
Data science is gated behind postgraduate degrees and it's the most crowded target in tech. AI engineering isn't the same job. It rewards people who can build and deploy things, and that is exactly the thing almost nobody coming off a course can do. You already ship — brewmaster-app.com is live with analytics on it. That's the whole gap, and you're already on the right side of it.

The reorder: most roadmaps make you do 40 hours of scikit-learn and 18 hours of PyTorch before you touch an LLM. But you'll rarely open PyTorch in this job. Doing AI engineering at week 4 instead of week 20 gets your flagship app live several months earlier — and the live app is what makes applications get answered. Core ML is backfill; it matters, but it isn't the gate.
The thing that gets you hired
Evals. Anyone can follow a LangChain tutorial and produce a chatbot. Very few can answer "how do you know it's any good?" — and that question is reportedly the strongest single signal of real LLM experience in interviews. Expect to be handed a corpus and asked how you'd chunk it, what embeddings you'd pick, whether you'd rerank, and how you'd measure the result.

The second underrated one is cost. Most engineers can build a working RAG pipeline; almost none can halve the production bill with quality flat. If you can show a token-spend graph with a caching change in the middle of it, that's a story nobody else in the pile is telling.

One app, not four repos. Let brewmaster grow: it gains retrieval, then evals, then a model, then monitoring. Depth reads far better than four disconnected tutorials, and "I ran this in production and here's what broke" is the sentence that gets you the offer.
